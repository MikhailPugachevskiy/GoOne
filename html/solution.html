The market is growing. The rider experience is not.

Bike-sharing is everywhere, but the customer journey is split across apps. There is no combined map, no price comparison, and no single account. Finding a bike still costs time.

Four frictions. One broken journey.

Riders do not lack bikes. They lack a single place to see them, compare them, pay once, and leave. 26.5% of surveyed users already juggle more than one app.

No aggregated overview

Each operator shows only its own fleet. The closest bike may sit in another app you never opened.

No price comparison

Fares stay hidden until you switch apps. Riders cannot scan the cheapest ride in one glance.

Time-consuming search

Opening, locating, and discarding apps turns a two-minute trip into a hunting exercise.

Different accounts

Every provider wants its own login, payment method, and lock flow. One city, several identities.

Why this hurts more than it looks

If four in ten users rent more than once a week, every extra tap is repeated. Fragmentation is not a one-time annoyance. It is a tax on daily mobility.
