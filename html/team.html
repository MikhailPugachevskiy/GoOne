Chapter 5 · Business

Customers, competition, and the path to break-even

Go One stands on two pillars: bike-sharing providers and their riders. We enter with an MVP, grow coverage, then raise two rounds of EUR 200,000 to reach break-even in 2021.

Two customer pillars. A clear competitive play.

Providers supply the bikes. Riders supply the demand. Competitors already try to sit in the middle. Go One wins by shipping an MVP first, locking in operator relationships, and offering one account instead of extra sign-ups.

Providers

German anchors such as Nextbike and Call a Bike, plus Mobike, LimeBike, Byke and Donkey Republic.

Riders

Young to mid-age urban users: about half are 25–44, 71% male, 65% with an academic background.

Competitors

Direct rivals are urbi and Free2Move. Bikeee is a low-functionality newcomer. Foreign apps have little German presence.

How we compete

Go to market with the MVP, foster provider partnerships, and raise usefulness with a single Go One account.

From MVP to European roll-out

The first product shows nearby bikes and redirects riders to the operator. Version 2.0 adds one-account booking. Europe is assumed to grow about 1.5% per month. Targets: up to 20% share in Germany and up to 5% in selected markets.
