Chapter 2 · Problem

The market is growing. The rider experience is not.

Bike-sharing is everywhere, but the customer journey is split across apps. There is no combined map, no price comparison, and no single account. Finding a bike still costs time.

Demand is already habitual

In our own survey (N = 104), 40.4% of bike-sharing users rent a bike more than once a week. That is not a one-off tourist product. It is weekly urban mobility.

High use frequency

Four in ten surveyed riders rent more than once a week, so switching costs between apps add up fast.

Investor attention

Alibaba, DST Global and other funds have already put more than USD 2.2 billion into the category.

Fragmented supply

Over 1,250 operators exist globally. Riders in one city often face several apps at once.

Perfect timing

Shared mobility is expanding beyond bikes into e-scooters. An aggregator can grow with the category.

What this boom means for Go One

Providers are multiplying. Capital is already in the market. Users ride often. The missing layer is not another operator. It is one map across all of them.
